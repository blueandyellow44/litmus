Legal, Disclosures & Corrections

Effective July 10, 2026

LITMUS reads news the way a critic reads it. For every story it shows two independent readings: where the writing leans politically, and how it is told, on a Practical-to-Hysterical scale. This page explains what those readings are, what they are not, how source material is used, and how to reach us to correct or remove something.

1. The scores are opinions, not facts

Every lean reading, every Practical-to-Hysterical score, every highlighted passage, every flagged rhetorical device, and every short "what happened" summary on LITMUS is generated by an automated system (an AI language model) and is offered as opinion and commentary, not as a statement of verified fact. These readings reflect an assessment of how a piece of writing is constructed. They are judgments, and reasonable readers may disagree with them.

LITMUS is not a fact-checker. It does not, and cannot, tell you whether any claim in an article is true or false. A low or high score is a comment on the writing, not on the honesty or character of any journalist, outlet, or person named in the story.

2. The basis is disclosed

The scores are opinions formed from a stated method, applied to the article's own words. Each article is assessed on five dimensions: headline framing, language intensity, source balance, logical fallacies, and structural manipulation. The Practical-to-Hysterical result falls in one of five bands, from Practical (wire-service-style reporting) to Hysterical (writing built to make you feel before it tells you anything). The full method is described on the About page. Where a score points to specific language, that language is shown alongside the judgment so you can weigh the reading against the evidence yourself.

3. How third-party articles are used

  • Names, not logos. Outlet and publication names appear only to identify whose article is being analyzed. LITMUS does not display outlet logos, and its use of these names is nominative: it does not imply any affiliation, sponsorship, or endorsement.
  • Short excerpts for criticism. Where article text is quoted, it is quoted in limited amounts for the purpose of criticism and commentary, with attribution and a prominent link to the original. The aim is analysis of the writing, not republication of it.
  • Read it at the source. LITMUS is designed to send you to the original article, not to replace it. Links to the source are provided throughout.
